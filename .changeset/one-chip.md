"audio90": minor
---

A chip is now one object, and plays songs itself. `createAudio80` and `createAudio90` return the chip, not `{ audio80, controls }` or `{ audio90, controls }`: what the controls did, `ready`, `pause`, `resume`, `isPaused`, `volume`, `isMuted`, `musicVolume`, `effectsVolume` and `destroy`, is on the chip, which has the type `SoundChip` in common. `AudioControls`, `HeadlessControls`, `Audio80WithControls` and `Audio90WithControls` are gone. `createMusicPlayer`, `MusicPlayer`, `MusicPlayerOptions`, `PlayableSong`, `SongSlot` and `openSongSlot` are gone too: `play(song)` starts a song on the chip, `queue(song)` plays one after it, and the chip has the song heard, its `orderIndex`, `row` and `beat`, and `tempoScale`. `play` returns an id for a song as for a sound, and `queue` returns the id of the play it follows. `stop(id)` stops that play, a sound or a song, and `stop()` with no id stops everything, in place of `releaseAll` and the controls' `reset`. While the chip is paused, `stop()` cuts every voice at once rather than letting them fade out as it resumes. The headless chips have one type each, whatever their options: `HeadlessAudio80` and `HeadlessAudio90` are the chip, with `update`, `log`, `clear`, `sampleRate` and `renderInto`, which throws on a chip made without `render`. `Audio80Log`, `Audio90Log`, `Audio80Renderer` and `Audio90Renderer` are gone, a stop with no id is logged with an `id` of `undefined`, and nothing is logged as `release-all`. `renderSong` and `renderSound` are one function, `render`.
